## Mission

Unlock the economic and social potential of "Bharat" — the roughly 900 million
Indians who live, work and transact in their native languages rather than in
English.

## What we build

A multi-product ecosystem rather than a single app: local news and civic
information, jobs and hiring, classifieds, matrimony, agricultural advisory,
edutainment, astrology, and peer support. Nine-plus products, one platform
foundation.

## Who we build for

Users in Tier 2 and Tier 3 towns and beyond — 250+ districts, 10+ Indian
languages, 60M+ users. Many are on low-end devices and intermittent connections,
and many are coming online for the first time. They are not the users most
engineers design for by default.

## Engineering reality

Engineers should expect, and be comfortable with:

- **Multi-product context switching.** Teams operate as small autonomous pods.
  People move between products. Depth in one vertical matters less than the
  ability to pick up an unfamiliar system quickly.
- **Cost-conscious infrastructure.** Instances are sized deliberately small.
  "Scale the box up" is available but is rarely the first answer. Engineers are
  expected to reason about what a system actually needs before adding capacity.
- **Consumer scale on a tight budget.** Traffic is large and margins are thin.
  Efficiency is a design constraint, not an afterthought.
- **Fast iteration.** Priorities shift. Scoping and sequencing judgment matters
  as much as implementation.
- **Real users, real consequences.** A bug does not inconvenience a SaaS
  dashboard; it affects someone's job application or crop advisory.

## What we value in people

- **Ownership over process.** Ship it, watch it, fix it.
- **Pragmatism over purity.** The right answer accounts for the constraint you
  actually have, not the one you wish you had.
- **Honesty about limits.** "I don't know, here is how I'd find out" is a strong
  answer. Confident bluffing is not.
- **Curiosity about the user.** The best engineers here can explain who uses the
  thing they built and why it matters to them.

## How to read this during an interview

This describes the environment, not a checklist. A candidate is not expected to
have worked somewhere like this before, and unfamiliarity with the context is
never a mark against them. Use it only to judge whether the way someone reasons
would translate here — and to keep scenarios realistic.
