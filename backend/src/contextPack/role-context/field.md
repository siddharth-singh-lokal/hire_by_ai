# Role context — Field, Sales & On-Ground roles

Hand-maintained and public-safe, like `company-profile.md`, and deliberately NOT
run through the sanitizer (see the note in `product.md`). No targets, no
commission structures, no pricing. Candidates are never quizzed on it.

Covers field sales, distribution and collections, delivery, on-ground operations,
retail counter roles and area supervision — the roles most often hired through a
jobs platform rather than a résumé.

## What these roles actually involve

Being out of the office, on a two-wheeler, covering an assigned area. Visiting
shops or customers, taking orders on a phone app, explaining what is on offer,
collecting payment, reconciling at day end. Alone for most of it, deciding things
on the spot with no manager present. The people they deal with are small business
owners and households under no obligation to be polite or consistent.

## What makes it harder than it sounds

- **Rejection is the baseline.** Most visits do not convert. Someone who cannot
  absorb that cheerfully will not last a month.
- **Money creates conflict.** Disputed bills, short payments, goods the customer
  says never arrived, cash that does not reconcile. Handling a disagreement about
  money without either caving or escalating everything is the central judgement.
- **Nobody is watching, which cuts both ways.** Route discipline, honest
  reporting and turning up are self-enforced. Integrity is a real criterion here.
- **Self-routed work differs from dispatched work.** Being handed a delivery is
  not the same skill as deciding which twenty-five shops to visit today and in
  what order. People often have the first and not the second.
- **The phone app is the system of record**, on a low-end Android with
  intermittent data.
- **Local language and local knowledge decide everything.**
- **Two-wheeler and licence are hard requirements** for most of these roles, as
  is living within reach of the territory.

## The kind of problem worth reasoning about

- A shop owner refuses to order because he disputes an old bill and the records
  are not to hand.
- Day-end cash does not match what the app says was collected.
- A loyal customer suddenly says they are switching to a competitor.
- The route cannot be finished in the time available and something must be
  dropped.
- A customer asks for something the worker is not authorised to promise.
- The app will not sync and the day's orders are stuck on the phone.

## How they are expected to work

- Ownership without supervision: plan the day, work it, report it honestly.
- Persistence that stays pleasant after being turned down.
- Straightforwardness about money and mistakes.
- Enough local judgement to decide on the spot, and enough sense to know which
  decisions are not theirs.
