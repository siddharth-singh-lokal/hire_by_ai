# Role context — Marketing & Growth

Hand-maintained and public-safe, like `company-profile.md`, and deliberately NOT
run through the sanitizer (see the note in `product.md`). No spend figures, no
CAC or ROAS numbers, no channel economics, no campaign results — those are the
business. Candidates are never quizzed on any of it; this exists so questions
look like the actual work.

Covers performance and growth marketing, user acquisition, retention and CRM,
brand, content and social.

## What a marketing person here actually works on

Growth across a house of separate consumer apps rather than one product — local
news and civic information, jobs, classifieds, matrimony, agricultural advisory,
astrology consultations, regional-language edutainment, peer support. Small
autonomous pods, so one person often covers more than one product and switches
between them.

The audience is the part that does not transfer from most marketing careers.

## Who they are marketing to

Users in Tier-2 and Tier-3 towns and smaller districts, across many Indian
languages, mostly on low-end Android phones and metered, intermittent data. Many
are coming online for the first time.

Several instincts from English-first, metro, high-ARPU marketing actively
mislead here:

- **Creative has to work in ten languages**, and a transliterated headline is
  not a translated one. Copy that is clever in English is often meaningless once
  localised.
- **Install is the cheap part; the first session is the hard part.** Acquisition
  that looks efficient can deliver users who never get past OTP or permissions.
- **Data cost and app size are conversion factors**, not engineering details.
- **Trust is the constraint.** For a jobs or matrimony product, users have been
  scammed before and are rightly suspicious of anything that overpromises.
- **Word of mouth and local community carry more weight than paid** in these
  markets, and are much harder to instrument.
- **Attribution is genuinely murky** on low-end devices with reinstalls and
  shared handsets.

## The kind of problem worth reasoning about

Situational, never a request for numbers they cannot know:

- A channel starts delivering installs at a good cost, but those users never
  reach the core action, and someone has to decide whether to keep spending.
- A campaign works in one language or one state and not in another.
- A creative performs well on a metric that turns out not to matter.
- Retention drops after a product change and it must be separated from a
  seasonal or acquisition-mix effect.
- Two products want the same audience and the same budget.
- Something needs launching in a language nobody on the team speaks.

## How they are expected to work

- Ownership over process: ship the campaign, watch it, fix it.
- Curiosity about the user — they can explain who they are talking to and why
  the message lands with that person, not just what the channel returned.
- Honesty about what a number does and does not establish.
- Pragmatism: the answer accounts for the constraint that exists, not the one
  they wish existed.
