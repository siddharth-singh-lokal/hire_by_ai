# Role context — Care, Support & Consultation roles

Hand-maintained and public-safe, like `company-profile.md`, and deliberately NOT
run through the sanitizer (see the note in `product.md`). No business logic, no
payout or pricing detail. Candidates are never quizzed on it; it exists so
questions look like the actual work.

Covers roles where the job **is** the conversation: peer-support listeners,
counsellors, consultation experts, telecallers, customer-support and screening
agents.

## What these roles actually involve

Talking to strangers by voice, on their phone, about something that matters to
them. The person may be lonely, anxious, confused about a service, or unsure
what they want. Work is paid by the minute or handled as a queue, usually from
the worker's own phone and their own chosen hours.

The measurable skill is **keeping a conversation alive and useful with someone
who is not helping you do it.**

## What makes it harder than it sounds

- **Silence is the default failure.** The commonest hard moment is a person who
  will not open up: one-word answers, "I don't know", long pauses. Handling that
  is the job, and it cannot be scripted.
- **The user often has no clear ask.** Working out what someone actually needs,
  without interrogating them, is the core competence.
- **Boundaries are the safety layer.** Requests to move off the platform, share
  personal contact details, or connect on social media are common and must be
  handled warmly but firmly. Not seeing why that matters is a risk to users.
- **Distress must be recognised, not treated.** These are not clinicians. The
  skill is noticing when something is beyond them and escalating.
- **One bad session is visible** — it costs a user's trust, and usually a refund
  and a complaint.
- **Low-end phones, patchy lines, many languages.** Users switch language
  mid-sentence; warmth in the user's own language matters far more than English.

## The kind of problem worth reasoning about

Situational, never trivia, never a test of clinical knowledge:

- Someone goes quiet minutes in and only says "I don't know" when asked how they
  feel, and there is still time on the call.
- A user asks for their personal number or social handle.
- A user proposes moving payment or contact off the platform.
- A user says something suggesting they may be in real danger.
- A user is angry about something the worker cannot fix.
- A caller wants a definite answer the worker is not qualified to give.

## How they are expected to work

- Patience with difficult people, without judgement.
- Honesty about limits — knowing when to escalate is a strength.
- Reliability: online during the hours they committed to.
- Warmth that survives a difficult caller, and firmness that survives a charming
  one.
